Summary

This is a real business with a validated offer. A 165 to 185 dollar faith watch is converting cold traffic at 95 dollar acquisition cost, the conviction creative is producing viral reach, and the bundle and upsell engine is lifting AOV to nearly 200 dollars. The account is not broken. It has been scaled roughly twentyfold in a single month and efficiency compressed toward breakeven as that happened.

The fix is not a new product or a new market. It is recovering efficiency at the current spend: consolidate a fragmented structure behind proven winners, seal two checkout-stage leaks, sharpen the top-funnel signal, and protect the ad account from policy risk so spend can compound rather than stall. The margin is already inside this funnel. The job is to stop leaking it.

What Has To Happen This Week

  • Consolidate the structure. Sixty-two active ad sets, a third of spend in a 124-ad long tail, plus Zombie ad sets at 1.3x to 1.5x. Concentrate budget into a clean Advantage+ plus testing plus retargeting frame.
  • Seal the checkout. Only 39.3 percent of initiated checkouts complete and only 25.7 percent of add to carts initiate checkout. This is the largest pool of recoverable revenue at current spend.
  • De-risk the creative. The angles that attack protected groups carry real Meta policy exposure. At 3,000 dollars a day an account restriction is catastrophic. Re-cut the conviction hook around faith identity, not attack.
  • Sharpen the signal. Lift PageView and View Content match quality from 6.4 toward 8 plus with richer customer-data parameters so the algorithm optimises on cleaner data.

Product Page, Cart & Checkout CRO

The CRO Picture: Strong Base, Two Fixable Drops

The store is well built. There is a quantity bundle selector, a reserved-cart urgency timer, a free adjustment tool, a shipping-protection bump, a premium-case upsell, an in-cart testimonial, broad payment methods, a one-year warranty and a 30-day money-back guarantee. This is a more advanced funnel than most stores at this spend. The work here is targeted, not a rebuild.

Product Page and Cart Observations

  • The email pop-up interrupts on entry. The "Statement Piece" modal with a guilt-decline appears immediately and covers the hero. Switch to a delayed or exit-intent trigger so paid visitors see the product before the interruption.
  • The cart drawer is doing a lot at once. Bundle tiers, a free tool, a shipping-protection toggle, a premium-case toggle and a testimonial all sit above the checkout button. This richness likely contributes to the steep add-to-cart to checkout drop. Make the Secure Checkout button unmissable and reduce the cognitive load above it.
  • The two-watch bundle is the AOV lever. Most orders are single watches. Default-select the two-watch tier, badge it as most chosen, and frame the saving in absolute dollars rather than only a struck-through price.
  • Surface review stars in the buy box. The collection page advertises 4.6 out of 5 from 7,000 plus believers, but that trust signal does not always reach the user at the decision point. Place an aggregate rating directly under the product title.
  • Quality expectations need managing. Public reviews praise durability and the Crown of Thorns finish, but a few flag a non-moving dial and a missing promised gift box. Tighten quality control and fulfilment promises, and pre-empt the movement question on the page, since unmet expectations drive refunds and dampen repeat.

Checkout Observations

  • Only 39.3 percent of checkouts complete. This is the single highest-value fix on the store. Everything below feeds it.
  • Express options are thin at the top. Checkout leads with Shop Pay and Google Pay only. Klarna is offered yet not surfaced as an express button, and PayPal is absent up top. Add Klarna and PayPal express to catch the buyers who prefer them.
  • Auto-added costs at checkout can suppress completion. Shipping plus an auto-toggled shipping-protection line appear at the final step. Test whether defaulting protection off, or making the free-shipping threshold explicit earlier, lifts completion.
  • Phone is optional. For SMS abandoned-cart recovery on a considered purchase, make phone required with a short reason, for delivery updates.
  • Trust stack is light in the order summary. Add a compact row of guarantees near the total: warranty, 30-day returns, secure payment, tracked shipping.
  • Carry urgency into checkout. The reserved-cart timer and same-day-dispatch message live on the product page but fade at the pay step, exactly where reassurance and urgency matter most.

Site Performance, Read Carefully

Real users currently pass Core Web Vitals in the field. Mobile landing is around 1.4 seconds and desktop around 1.5 seconds, with good interaction and layout-shift scores. That is why the funnel works. The lab picture is the warning sign. Lighthouse scores the product page at 24 on mobile and 28 on desktop, dragged down by a 12 to 14 megabyte page payload, roughly 900 kilobytes of unused JavaScript, render-blocking requests and heavy main-thread work. As paid mobile traffic scales onto slower devices and connections, that weight becomes a conversion tax and part of the 28 percent of clicks that never become a landing page view. Trimming the payload, deferring unused scripts and setting explicit image dimensions is incremental conversion upside, not an emergency.

Hook is the three-second view rate, hold is the share still watching at the through point. Conviction ads earn the click but lose a high share before the message lands, which is why the bucket ROAS sits below the account average even though it produces the standout winners.

What Is Working

  • Unboxing returns the most per dollar. At 2.23x on 19,500 dollars it beats every other engine on efficiency and answers the only real question a 165 dollar online watch raises, which is whether it looks and feels real.
  • Conviction voiceover produces the biggest individual winners. The top ad by return, a cultural-contrast voiceover, ran 3,948 dollars at 2.77x and a 67 dollar cost per purchase off a 63 percent hook.
  • The batch ad sets concentrate the winners well. The top set, Batch INT all winning ads, did 14,994 dollars at 2.47x across 186 purchases. The Unboxings batch did 7,254 dollars at 2.64x. These are the templates to feed.
  • Two standout single ads beat the account. Ad2 returned 4.03x and INT Crazy1 returned 3.37x, both well clear of the 2.08x blended figure and worth isolating for scale.

What Is Dragging

  • Conviction hold rate is too low. A 45 percent hook with a 17 percent hold means most of the paid attention drops before the product or offer appears. The fix is structural: get the watch on screen earlier.
  • Zombie sets are bleeding efficiency. Zombie WW at 1.33x and Zombie USA and CAN at 1.54x both sit below the 1.68x breakeven floor. These are clear pause-or-rebuild candidates.
  • The tail is enormous and idle. 141 of 260 active ads have spent under 50 dollars and 151 have zero purchases. The account is carrying a large dormant library that dilutes learning and clutters delivery.
  • Policy exposure sits on the biggest winners. Several top conviction ads lean on culture-war framing that attacks protected groups. That is a live account-restriction risk at 3,000 dollars a day and a fragile base to scale on. The durable path is positive faith identity, gifting and craft, which we expand in the concepts section.
The constraint is refresh speed, not fatigue. Weekly frequency is only 1.24 and CPM is flat near 13 dollars, so the audience is not burned. What limits the account is how quickly proven structures get rebuilt into fresh variations. With 43 ads carrying 80 percent of spend, a disciplined weekly cycle of three to five new variants per winning engine, built from the anatomy on the next slide, keeps delivery fed without touching reach.

The Plan

Restore Efficiency, Then Scale

The issue is sequencing, not survival. Scaling further on a thin-margin, fragmented base only buys more unprofitable volume. The right order is to fix the conversion leaks and tighten the structure first, lift the return on the spend already running, and only then push budget hard onto a base that can hold it. Three phases.

Phase One
Days 1 to 30, Stabilise
  • Ship the checkout and cart fixes that target the two critical leaks
  • Consolidate 62 ad sets into the Advantage+ core, testing and retargeting frame
  • Pause Zombie sets and the 151 zero-sale ads
  • Stand up the retargeting campaign on the existing healthy pixel
  • Hold spend near current levels while efficiency recovers
Goal: lift blended ROAS back above 2.0x without adding spend.
Phase Two
Days 31 to 90, Rebuild Creative
  • Launch the two priority concepts, Heirloom Gift and Craft Answers the Doubt
  • Run the weekly cadence of three to five brand-safe variants
  • Front-load product and craft in conviction cuts to lift hold
  • Promote winners into the Advantage+ core, retire the policy-risk base
  • Begin measured budget steps as efficiency holds
Goal: a durable, policy-safe creative engine feeding a stable core.
Phase Three
Days 91 plus, Scale
  • Scale spend toward and beyond 5,000 dollars a day on the proven base
  • Hold ROAS at or above the 2.5x target with the guardrail in place
  • Push AOV toward 220 dollars through the bundle default and value framing
  • Layer seasonal pushes, Father's Day then Christmas, on the gifting concepts
  • Keep the weekly Ad Library sweep and creative refresh running
Goal: 8,000 dollars a day at a sustainable 2.5x.

In Short

Elior is a fast-scaled account that ran ahead of its operations. Demand is already proven: the brand has a message that resonates, creative that wins, and an efficient base period that confirms the unit economics. What is missing is the operational discipline underneath it. Fix the two checkout leaks, consolidate the structure, and rebuild the creative onto a policy-safe base, in that order. Do that and the account recovers its efficiency on the spend already running, then scales from roughly 3,000 dollars a day at a thin margin to a steady 2.5x at 8,000 dollars a day. The upside is real and it is reachable with the levers already inside the business.
